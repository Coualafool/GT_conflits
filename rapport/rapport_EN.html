01

What it is

plain-English summary

This folder is not a single document — it is the working home of a complete pedagogical kit designed to help a teacher walk a class through the Israeli–Palestinian conflict without reducing it to a binary, emotional or decontextualised quarrel. It is built and maintained by a volunteer working group (the folder calls it “GT conflits internationaux”) made up of educators, international-law jurists and NGO actors in francophone Belgium, with expert interventions logged from 2025 into 2026.

The material is explicitly positioned as a peace-education resource grounded in international law and critical thinking. Its own introduction sets the editorial tone (translated):

From the kit’s own introduction “As actors for peace, because we choose the side of Humanity, we refuse to give credence to one violence over another. We forcefully denounce the barbaric acts carried out by the Hamas terrorists on 7 October 2023, just as forcefully as we denounce the disproportionate violence against the civilians of Gaza by the Netanyahu government… We will not ‘rank the dead’.”

03

The 12-chapter core

the actual teaching sequence

The heart of the kit is a deliberately ordered narrative: it starts wide (the world order), narrows to the territory and its history, then interrogates language, law and media, before ending on what blocks peace and what a citizen can do. This is its strongest pedagogical idea and the spine any new toolset should keep.

#ChapterWhat it teaches
1L’ordre mondial aujourd’hui
The world order today		Bipolar → unipolar → multipolar world; who writes the rules; why the conflict is a lens on global power imbalances.
2Géopolitique d’Israël et de la Palestine
Geopolitics of Israel & Palestine		Comparative “identity card”: geography, institutions, economy, society, army/security; the Israel–USA couple; asymmetric balance of power.
3Ressources naturelles
Natural resources		Water (unequal access, water as a political tool) and gas (Gaza Marine field, naval blockade); what international law says.
4Historique du conflit
History of the conflict		From the rise of Zionism, WW1 promises (Hussein–McMahon, Sykes–Picot, Balfour), 1947–49 & the Nakba, 1967, Intifadas, Oslo, up to 7 October 2023.
5Le mot “conflit”
Qualifying the word “conflict”		Power of words (“terrorist”), propaganda’s 10 rules (Anne Morelli), legal categories (international vs non-international armed conflict), UN & ICJ 2004/2024 rulings.
6Pourquoi parle-t-on de génocide ?
Why speak of genocide?		1948 Convention definition, competent courts (ICJ/ICC), state prevention duties, the Gaza case (material & intent elements), 2023–25 timeline.
7Instrumentalisation du droit international
Instrumentalisation of international law		IHL principles, ICJ/ICC seizures, third-state obligations, structural weaknesses, self-defence debate, Ukraine-vs-Palestine asymmetry.
8Résonance médiatique
Media resonance		How events reach us; construction of the “enemy”; de-historicisation; dehumanising language; disinformation precedents; how social media & attention economies work; reclaiming critical thinking.
9Points clés qui bloquent une solution
What blocks a political solution		Borders/territory, status of Jerusalem, refugees’ right of return, settlements — each presented in three positions (Palestinian / Israeli / international).
10Les processus de paix
Peace processes		Oslo I/II, Wye River, Camp David II & Taba, Roadmap, Geneva Initiative, Trump “Deal of the Century” (2020), role of women (UNSC 1325), NY conference (Jul 2025), Sharm (Oct 2025).
11Agir
Act		Negative vs positive peace; Belgium as actor (arms exports, EU trade, 12 Belgian sanctions Sept 2025, 2026 royal decree on overflight); concrete citizen actions; inspiring initiatives (EcoPeace, Breaking the Silence, etc.).

How it was made

the working-group model

The GT conflits internationaux folder reveals a healthy production process: dated meeting minutes (P.V) from 2025 into 2026, each built around an invited expert. This is the kit’s hidden engine — and the thing worth preserving and formalising as it scales.

Expert-driven content

Minutes record interventions on media representation of genocide, international law, and civil-society perspectives — content is sourced from specialists, not written in isolation.

Iterative, versioned

Multiple “skeleton” versions (2019, 2025, V2) and a 2009 ancestor show a project that has been revised across years and crises — but versioning is currently done by filenames, not a real system.

06

Strengths & gaps

honest read of the current corpus

What’s strong

✔ A coherent, non-binary, law-grounded narrative spine.
✔ Expert-sourced and genuinely multi-angle (Palestinian / Israeli / international positions).
✔ A rare, transferable media & critical-thinking chapter.
✔ Built-in empathy/discussion method and honest acknowledgement of its own limits.
✔ Structured reference data (dates, lexicon) already exists.

What limits it as a “toolset”

✖ Teacher-facing prose only — nothing student-facing or interactive.
✖ Linear & one-size — no differentiation by age/level or learner role.
✖ Fast-rotting — the intro itself admits the news outpaces the document.
✖ Versioning by filename; no release process, no assessment, no accessibility.
✖ Single language, single country frame (Belgium), no offline/print-pack distribution.
✖ Sensitive advocacy stance needs explicit classroom guardrails to be defensible everywhere.

07

The proposal: a new toolset

from a folder of docs to a modular product

The goal is not to rewrite the content — it is good — but to re-express the same corpus as a modular, multi-audience toolset. Nine components, each mapped to the existing asset it reuses. Keep the 12-chapter spine as the shared “core”; wrap it in three layers (student, teacher, living) and one foundation (governance).

BuildReuses: 11 chapters + dates.xlsx

A · Student interactive core

Turn each chapter into a short interactive web module: a draggable timeline (from the key-dates spreadsheet), a swipe comparison of the Israel/Palestine “identity card”, a water-access data visualisation, and a hover glossary (from the Lexicon). Students navigate, not just read.

BuildReuses: pistes pédas + construire des ponts + Animations

B · Teacher facilitation kit

Formalise the “membrane of discussion” and the empathy exercise into a lesson plan + facilitation protocol + printable worksheets + slide decks. The Animations folder becomes ready-to-run activities; existing PPTX decks become the visual base.

BuildReuses: chapters 4,6,10 dated events

C · Living current-affairs layer

Split the corpus into a stable core (history, geography, law, concepts) and a live layer (ICJ 2024, NY conference Jul 2025, Trump plan Oct 2025, Belgian sanctions, 2026 decree). A versioned “what changed” feed keeps the kit from rotting — directly answering the intro’s own worry.

BuildReuses: all chapters, 3 registers

D · Differentiation tracks

Same core, three registers: lower-secondary (concepts, map, timeline, guided empathy), upper-secondary (law, media, positions), adult / NGO (full depth, current affairs). One content source, three outputs.

BuildReuses: chapter 8 (most transferable)

E · Standalone media & information-literacy lab

Package chapter 8 as a conflict-agnostic workshop: propaganda’s 10 rules, algorithmic bubbles, fact-checking drills. Usable for any current event — broadens the audience far beyond this single conflict.

BuildReuses: chapter 11 + empathy method

F · Assessment & reflection

Light formative quizzes per module + a reflection journal (the kit already leans on empathy and “defending the other’s view”). Measures understanding without grading opinions.

BuildHandles: sensitivity

G · Editorial charter, safeguarding & access

A one-page editorial charter + facilitator code for polarised classrooms, content notes, WCAG accessibility, and multilingual delivery (FR primary → NL for Belgium → EN for wider reach). Keeps the documented facts intact while making the kit defensible across diverse classrooms.

BuildReuses: whole corpus

H · Delivery & distribution

Ship as a small static site / web app + a printable PDF pack + an offline bundle (the 2009 kit shipped on CD — same instinct, modern medium). Open licence (CC BY-NC-SA) so teachers adapt it; light analytics to see what’s used.

BuildReuses: GT working-group model

I · Governance & production pipeline

Keep the expert working group, but add a repeatable pipeline: expert review → pedagogical translation → student pilot → versioned release; a real reference/citation manager (the docs already cite sources); and a fixed maintenance cadence for the live layer. This turns a heroic volunteer effort into a sustainable one.

09

Risks & guardrails

this is sensitive material

Editorial defensibility

The kit takes an explicit advocacy stance (“side of Humanity”, condemns all violence). For schools, pair that with transparent sourcing, the existing three-positions structure, and a review by relevant education authorities — present documented facts rigorously without sanitising them.

Classroom safety

Polarisation and student distress are real. The facilitation kit (B) and safeguarding note (G) must give teachers scripts, content notes, and a way to hold the “membrane of discussion” without exposing students.

Currency & accuracy

Legal and current-affairs claims (ICJ/ICC, sanctions, decrees) age fast. The living layer (C) needs named sources, dates, and a review owner — otherwise the kit quietly becomes wrong.

Sustainability

Volunteer working groups burn out. The governance pipeline (I), open licence, and modular design let others contribute without a single point of failure.

Glossary

domain terms in plain English
Outil pédagogique / Mallette pédagogique
A packaged teaching kit — here, the whole resource set built for teachers.
GT (Groupe de travail)
“Working group” — the volunteer team producing and reviewing the content.
P.V. (Procès-verbal)
Minutes of a meeting — the working group’s logged discussions and expert inputs.
CLC
The originating course/committee framework the project grew from (seen in the 2009 & 2019 “skeleton” files).
Squelette
“Skeleton” — the project’s own outline/plan before full drafting.
DI / DIH
Droit international / DI humanitaire — international law / international humanitarian law.
CIJ / CPI
The International Court of Justice (ICJ) and International Criminal Court (ICC).
Nakba
“Catastrophe” — the 1948 destruction/displacement of Palestinian society during the creation of Israel.
UNRWA
The UN agency for Palestinian refugees.
Negative vs positive peace
Negative = absence of war; positive = presence of justice, equality and the conditions that prevent conflict (a key idea in chapter 11).
WCAG
Web Content Accessibility Guidelines — the standard for making digital tools usable by people with disabilities.
CC BY-NC-SA
Creative Commons licence: share/adapt with attribution, non-commercial, same-licence-alike — lets teachers legally remix the kit.
